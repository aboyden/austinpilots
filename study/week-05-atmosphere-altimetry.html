Day 1

The Atmosphere, Heat, and Pressure

Topics: AWH Chapters 4–6 · About 60 min
Learning objectives
  • Describe the layers of the atmosphere relevant to flight
  • Explain how heat imbalances between the equator and poles drive circulation

🌍 The atmosphere in layers IR.I.B.K3a

Dry air is about 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen. Water vapor is a small and highly variable share, but it drives most of the weather you will plan around.

LayerWhat to know
TroposphereSurface to roughly 36,000 ft at mid-latitudes (lower at the poles, higher at the equator). Nearly all flying, and all weather, happens here.
TropopauseThe boundary where temperature stops dropping with altitude. Its altitude, and the jet stream that rides near it, shift with latitude and season.
StratosphereAbove the tropopause; temperature is stable or even increases with height.

📏 Standard atmosphere values worth memorizing

QuantityStandard value
Sea-level pressure29.92 in. Hg / 1013.25 mb
Sea-level temperature15°C / 59°F
Lapse rate2°C (3.5°F) per 1,000 ft, up to the tropopause
Pressure changeRoughly 1 in. Hg per 1,000 ft in the lower atmosphere (the approximation breaks down as air thins)

🔥 Why heat imbalance matters IR.I.B.K3c

The equator receives far more direct solar heating than the poles, and that imbalance is the engine behind global circulation, jet streams, and most of the pressure systems you will read about in Week 6. Air does not simply flow from hot to cold; the Coriolis effect deflects it (right in the Northern Hemisphere, left in the Southern), producing the semi-permanent pressure belts and prevailing wind patterns that show up on prog charts.

🌡️ Worked example

Climbing through 34,000 ft

The outside air temperature stops dropping and levels off even though you keep climbing.

You have likely just reached the tropopause for that latitude and day. Above it, in the stratosphere, temperature behaves very differently from the steady lapse rate you were taught to expect below.
Self-check
  1. What is the standard lapse rate, and up to what layer does it generally apply?
  2. What are the standard sea-level pressure and temperature values?
  3. What ultimately drives global atmospheric circulation?
Show answers
  1. 2°C (3.5°F) per 1,000 ft, up to the tropopause.
  2. 29.92 in. Hg (1013.25 mb) and 15°C (59°F).
  3. The heat imbalance between the equator (more direct solar heating) and the poles (less), deflected by the Coriolis effect.

Day 2

Altimetry: Pressure and Density Altitude

Topics: AWH Chapters 7–9 · About 60 min
Learning objectives
  • Calculate pressure altitude and density altitude from given conditions
  • Explain why high, hot, and humid conditions degrade aircraft performance

📐 Pressure altitude and density altitude IR.II.B.K1a

Pressure altitude is your indicated altitude corrected to what it would read with the altimeter set to 29.92. For every 1 in. Hg the current altimeter setting is below 29.92, add 1,000 ft to field elevation (or indicated altitude); for every 1 in. Hg above 29.92, subtract 1,000 ft.

Density altitude is pressure altitude further corrected for non-standard temperature: it goes up when it is hotter than standard for that pressure altitude and down when colder. Higher density altitude means thinner air, which means less lift, less propeller and engine efficiency, and longer takeoff and landing rolls.

Humidity has a smaller but real effect in the same direction as heat: moist air is slightly less dense than dry air at the same temperature and pressure, because water vapor molecules are lighter than the nitrogen and oxygen they displace.

Rule of thumb
Density altitude ≈ pressure altitude + 120 ft for every °C the temperature is above standard for that pressure altitude. Your flight computer or an online calculator gives a more precise number.
🌡️ Worked example

High, hot, and humid

Field elevation 5,000 ft, altimeter setting 29.42, temperature 30°C.

This is the classic hot-high-humid performance trap: a 5,000-ft airport performing like one at nearly 9,000 ft.
Two classic altimeter traps
Pressure: flying from high pressure toward low pressure without resetting the altimeter, the altimeter reads higher than your true altitude (“high to low, look out below”). Temperature: in air colder than standard, the altimeter also reads higher than true altitude, which matters for terrain and obstacle clearance on cold days.
Self-check
  1. Altimeter setting is 30.42. Is pressure altitude higher or lower than indicated altitude, and by how much?
  2. Does density altitude increase or decrease as temperature rises above standard for a given pressure altitude?
  3. Why does higher density altitude hurt aircraft performance?
Show answers
  1. Lower, by 500 ft (30.42 is 0.50 in. Hg above 29.92, so subtract 500 ft).
  2. Increase.
  3. The air is thinner, producing less lift and less engine/propeller efficiency, which lengthens takeoff and landing distances and reduces climb performance.
