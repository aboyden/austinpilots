Day 1

VOR Orientation

Topics: IFH Chapter 7 (VOR) · About 70 min
Learning objectives
  • Determine your position relative to a VOR from OBS, CDI, and TO/FROM indications
  • Explain the difference between a radial and a course

📡 The basics IR.II.B.K2a IR.V.A.K1

A VOR radiates 360 usable radials, numbered by their magnetic direction from the station. Your receiver compares the OBS-selected course to the signal and shows a TO or FROM flag and left/right needle deflection. TO means the selected course, if flown, would take you to the station; FROM means it would take you away. A radial is always defined outbound from the station; the course you fly TO the station on that same line is the reciprocal.

🧩 Working an orientation problem

OBS set to 090 and the needle is centered with...You are on...Flying heading 090 takes you...
FROM flagThe 090 radial (east of the station)Away from the station
TO flagThe 270 radial (west of the station)Toward the station

If the needle is off center, the direction it deflects tells you which way to turn to reach the selected course, not which side of the station you are on in compass terms. Always relate the needle back to the selected course, never to your heading. Full-scale deflection is 10° either side, and each dot is 2°.

VOR accuracy check (91.171)
VOR equipment used for IFR must be checked within the preceding 30 days. Allowable error is ±4° on a VOT or ground checkpoint, ±6° for an airborne checkpoint, and no more than 4° between two VOR receivers checked against each other. Record the date, place, bearing error, and your signature.
🧭 Worked example

OBS 090, needle centered, FROM

You are on the 090 radial, east of the station, and flying heading 090 would take you further away.

To fly to the station from here you would fly 270, the reciprocal. Set the OBS to 270 and the flag reads TO. Try the archived VOR Navigation module for interactive orientation practice.
Self-check
  1. OBS centered with a FROM flag. What is your relationship to the selected course?
  2. Is a “radial” defined inbound or outbound from the station?
  3. If the CDI needle is deflected, what does the direction of deflection tell you?
Show answers
  1. You are on that course/radial, and flying it would take you away from the station.
  2. Outbound.
  3. Which way to turn to fly toward the selected course, relative to your current position, not a compass direction.

Day 2

DME and NDB/ADF

Topics: IFH Chapter 7 (DME, NDB/ADF) · About 60 min
Learning objectives
  • Explain what DME measures and how it differs from ground distance
  • Read a basic ADF relative bearing indication

📏 DME: slant range, not ground distance IR.II.B.K2a

DME measures the time for a signal to travel to a ground station and back, giving slant range: the straight line from your aircraft to the station. At low altitude and long range the difference is negligible. Directly over the station at altitude it reads your height in nautical miles rather than zero, a common test trap (6,000 ft AGL reads about 1 NM).

DME arcs
An arc is a path at a constant DME distance. If the DME reading is increasing, you are drifting outside the arc, so turn toward the station; if it is decreasing, turn away. The ACS standard is to hold the arc within ±1 NM IR.V.A.K1.

📻 NDB and ADF

A non-directional beacon transmits in all directions. The automatic direction finder (ADF) points its needle at the station regardless of aircraft heading, showing relative bearing: degrees clockwise from the nose to the station. To get the magnetic bearing to the station:

Magnetic Bearing = Magnetic Heading + Relative Bearing (subtract 360 if the total exceeds it)

NDB/ADF is older and increasingly rare in the US, but the ACS still covers it and it shows up on the test. Known ADF errors include thunderstorm interference (the needle can point toward lightning), night effect, coastal refraction, and mountain effect.

🧭 Worked example

Heading and relative bearing

Magnetic heading 040, ADF relative bearing 090 (needle off the right wingtip).

Magnetic bearing to the station = 040 + 090 = 130. If the sum had exceeded 360 you would subtract 360.
Self-check
  1. Why can DME read a nonzero distance directly over the station at altitude?
  2. What does an ADF needle actually indicate relative to the aircraft?
  3. Magnetic heading 270, ADF relative bearing 200. What is the magnetic bearing to the station?
Show answers
  1. DME measures slant range (straight-line distance), which at altitude directly over the station equals your altitude in miles, not zero.
  2. Relative bearing: degrees clockwise from the nose to the station.
  3. 270 + 200 = 470, minus 360 = 110.

Day 3

ILS and Marker Beacons

Topics: IFH Chapter 7 (ILS) · About 60 min
Learning objectives
  • Name the components of an ILS and their frequency bands
  • Identify each marker beacon by color and tone

🛬 ILS components IR.II.B.K2a IR.VI.B.K3

Localizer (lateral)

VHF, 108.10–111.95 MHz on the odd tenths (108.10, 108.30, …, 111.95). It shares the low end of the VOR band. Identifies in Morse code with an “I” before the three letters. Full scale is about 2.5° either side.

Glideslope (vertical)

UHF, 329.15–335.00 MHz. Automatically paired with the selected localizer frequency, so you never tune it directly. Typical glidepath about 3°. Full scale is about 0.7° either side.

Approach lighting

Extends visual guidance beyond the runway; also a visual reference you can use under 91.175(c).

Marker beacons or DME

Mark positions along the approach. Many modern installations substitute DME or GPS fixes for one or more markers.

🔵 Marker beacons

All three transmit on 75 MHz and are told apart by light color and tone:

MarkerLightTonePosition
OuterBlue400 Hz, dashesNear the glideslope intercept / final approach fix
MiddleAmber1300 Hz, alternating dots and dashesRoughly the decision-height position on a Category I ILS
InnerWhite3000 Hz, dotsUsed on Category II approaches
Category I
A Category I ILS has a decision height no lower than 200 ft above the touchdown zone, which is why the middle marker sits roughly where you decide.
🧭 Worked example

Tuning 109.9

You tune 109.9 for the localizer and the glideslope needle comes alive without touching a second frequency selector.

That is expected: each of the 40 localizer/glideslope frequency pairs is linked, so selecting the localizer selects the correct glideslope too. Always confirm the Morse ident before relying on the signal.
Self-check
  1. What frequency band does the localizer use, and what does it share that band with?
  2. What color light and tone identify the outer marker?
  3. What color light identifies the middle marker, and what does it approximate on a Category I ILS?
Show answers
  1. VHF, 108.10–111.95 MHz on odd tenths, shared with the low end of the VOR frequency band.
  2. Blue light, 400 Hz tone in dashes.
  3. Amber; it approximates the decision-height position.

Day 4

RNAV, GPS, WAAS, and RAIM

Topics: IFH Chapter 7 (RNAV/GPS) · About 60 min
Learning objectives
  • Rank LPV, LNAV/VNAV, LNAV, and LP by accuracy and equipment requirement
  • Explain what RAIM checks and when it matters

🛰️ The RNAV approach hierarchy IR.II.B.K2b IR.V.A.K2

Line of minimumsGuidanceMinimum is a...Needs
LPVLateral and verticalDA (as low as 200 ft AGL, like a Category I ILS)WAAS only
LNAV/VNAVLateral and verticalDA (higher than LPV)WAAS, or an approach-approved baro-VNAV system
LNAVLateral onlyMDAAny IFR-approved GPS with RAIM
LPLateral only (localizer performance)MDAWAAS only

LP is published where terrain or obstacles prevent a vertically guided LPV. LP and LPV are independent approach types, not fail-down modes of each other.

🔍 RAIM IR.II.B.R5

Receiver Autonomous Integrity Monitoring is the check a non-WAAS GPS performs to confirm enough satellites are available for the required accuracy. When you rely on GPS without WAAS, RAIM availability must be confirmed for the approach. WAAS-equipped aircraft do not need a separate RAIM check, since WAAS provides its own, more stringent integrity monitoring. Either way, the navigation database must be current for an approach.

🧭 Worked example

A WAAS outage before the approach

You briefed an LPV approach with a DA of 300 ft, but a WAAS outage is reported before you begin.

You cannot fly the LPV line of minimums without WAAS. You would step down to the LNAV line (an MDA, not a DA) if your GPS still has RAIM available, changing both the type of minimum and the descent technique.
Self-check
  1. Which RNAV approach type is WAAS-only and comparable in accuracy to a Category I ILS?
  2. Which RNAV approach type provides only lateral guidance and uses an MDA, requiring just RAIM (no WAAS)?
  3. Is LP a fail-down mode of LPV?
  4. Do WAAS-equipped aircraft need a separate RAIM check?
Show answers
  1. LPV.
  2. LNAV.
  3. No; LP and LPV are independent approach types, though both use WAAS.
  4. No; WAAS provides its own integrity monitoring, more stringent than RAIM.
