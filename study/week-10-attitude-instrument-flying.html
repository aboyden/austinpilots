Day 1

Control and Performance, Primary and Supporting

Topics: IFH Chapter 4 · About 60 min
Learning objectives
  • Explain the control-and-performance concept of instrument flying
  • Identify the primary pitch, bank, and power instrument in straight-and-level flight and in a constant-airspeed climb

🎛️ Control and performance IR.IV.A.K2

Attitude instrument flying works by setting control instruments (attitude and power) to produce a desired performance, which you confirm on the performance instruments (altimeter, airspeed indicator, heading indicator, VSI, turn coordinator). You set an attitude and a power setting, cross-check the performance instruments, and then adjust attitude and power. You do not chase the performance instruments directly.

GroupInstruments
ControlAttitude indicator, manifold pressure / RPM (power)
PerformanceAltimeter, airspeed indicator, heading indicator, VSI, turn coordinator

🥇 Primary and supporting instruments IR.IV.A.K1

For each phase of flight, one instrument gives the most direct feedback for each of pitch, bank, and power. That is the primary instrument for that axis; the others are supporting.

ManeuverPitchBankPower
Straight and levelAltimeterHeading indicatorAirspeed indicator
Level standard-rate turnAltimeterTurn coordinatorAirspeed indicator
Constant-airspeed climbAirspeed indicatorHeading indicatorManifold pressure / tachometer
Constant-airspeed descentAirspeed indicatorHeading indicatorManifold pressure / tachometer
Constant-rate climb or descentVSIHeading indicatorAirspeed indicator

The logic: whatever you are holding fixed is what you watch for that axis. In level flight you hold altitude, so the altimeter is primary for pitch, and with altitude fixed, power is what controls airspeed. In a constant-airspeed climb you hold airspeed with pitch, and power is set to a fixed value.

The three instrument skills
Instrument cross-check (scan), instrument interpretation (what each reading means), and aircraft control (the correct, smooth input). The classic cross-check errors are fixation (staring at one instrument), omission (leaving one out), and emphasis (relying on the one you like best instead of the primary one).
🧭 Worked example

Leveling off from a climb

Leveling off at your target altitude, you hold the climb attitude a beat too long and start to balloon above the altitude.

The fix is not to stare at the VSI. Adjust pitch attitude using the attitude indicator, then confirm on the altimeter (now your primary pitch instrument again in level flight) that you have captured the altitude.
Self-check
  1. In straight-and-level cruise flight, which instrument is primary for pitch?
  2. In a constant-airspeed climb, which instrument is primary for pitch?
  3. What is the core idea of the control-and-performance concept?
Show answers
  1. The altimeter.
  2. The airspeed indicator.
  3. Set attitude and power (the control instruments) to produce a desired performance, then cross-check the performance instruments to confirm, adjusting attitude and power rather than chasing the performance gauges directly.
