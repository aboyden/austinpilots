Day 1

Air Masses and Fronts

Topics: AWH Chapter 11 · About 70 min
Learning objectives
  • Describe the four front types and their typical weather
  • Explain the wave cyclone model in broad strokes

🌀 The four front types IR.I.B.K3e

FrontWhat is happeningTypical weather
ColdCold air displacing warm air; steep frontal slopeA narrow band of often intense weather (thunderstorms, gusty winds) that passes relatively quickly.
WarmWarm air overrunning retreating cold air; shallow frontal slopeA wide band of stratiform clouds and steady precipitation well ahead of the surface position; slower to pass.
StationaryLittle to no movementWeather similar to a warm front's leading edge, but it can persist for days.
OccludedA fast cold front catches a slower warm front and lifts it off the surfaceA cold-type occlusion behaves like a cold front, a warm-type like a warm front; either can mix both fronts' weather.

🔄 The wave cyclone model

A mid-latitude low pressure system typically forms along a stationary front where a wave develops. Then a cold front rotates around and eventually catches the warm front, occluding it and gradually dissipating the system. This model is the backbone for reading a surface analysis chart: find the low, trace the cold and warm fronts coming off it, and you can predict roughly what weather sits where relative to your route.

🧭 Worked example

Crossing a front two hours after departure

Expect different things depending on the front type.

Cold
A relatively narrow band of more intense weather (possible thunderstorms, turbulence, a wind shift) right at the crossing, with better conditions on both sides.
Warm
A much wider band of lower ceilings and steady precipitation well before you actually reach the surface frontal position.
Self-check
  1. Which front type typically produces a narrow band of more intense weather that passes quickly?
  2. Which front type produces a wide band of stratiform clouds and steady precipitation well ahead of its surface position?
  3. What happens to a warm front when a faster-moving cold front catches it?
Show answers
  1. The cold front.
  2. The warm front.
  3. It occludes: the cold front lifts the warm front off the surface, forming an occluded front.

Day 2

Stability, Clouds, and Precipitation

Topics: AWH Chapters 12–14 · About 70 min
Learning objectives
  • Explain what makes an air mass stable or unstable
  • Match cloud families to the weather and turbulence they usually signal

⚖️ Stability in a sentence IR.I.B.K3a

An air mass is unstable when a parcel of air, once lifted, continues to rise on its own (it is warmer, and therefore less dense, than the surrounding air at that altitude). It is stable when a lifted parcel resists rising and sinks back.

Unstable airStable air
CloudsTowering, cumuliformLayered, stratiform
PrecipitationShowerySteady
RideTurbulentSmoother
VisibilityGood between showersPoorer: fog, haze, and smoke stay trapped near the surface

☁️ Reading cloud families IR.I.B.K3f IR.I.B.K3d

Cumulus family (cumulus, cumulonimbus)

Signals instability, vertical development, and turbulence; potentially icing and thunderstorms in cumulonimbus.

Stratus family (stratus, nimbostratus)

Signals stability, layered structure, and steady precipitation with generally smoother air, though icing is still possible in the cloud layer.

Cirrus family

High, thin, ice-crystal clouds that are not usually a hazard by themselves, but can signal an approaching front or jet stream activity.

What lifts the air?

Convection (surface heating), orographic lift (terrain), frontal lifting, and convergence. Whichever lifts it, stability decides whether the lifted air keeps rising.

🌥️ Worked example

Building cumulus and a bumpy report

You see towering cumulus building rapidly along your route, and another aircraft nearby reports a bumpy ride.

Cumuliform clouds plus reported turbulence is a classic unstable air-mass signature. Expect the bumps to continue and watch for further vertical development toward thunderstorms, which is next week's topic.
Self-check
  1. What makes an air mass unstable, in terms of a lifted parcel's temperature?
  2. Which cloud family signals instability and vertical development?
  3. Which cloud family is associated with steady precipitation and generally smoother air?
Show answers
  1. The lifted parcel stays warmer (less dense) than the surrounding air, so it keeps rising on its own.
  2. The cumulus family (cumulus, cumulonimbus).
  3. The stratus family (stratus, nimbostratus).
