Day 1

METAR and TAF

Topics: AWH Chapters 24–25 · About 70 min
Learning objectives
  • Decode a METAR and a TAF without reference material
  • Identify the ceiling layer in a METAR/TAF sky condition group

📋 METAR: a snapshot IR.I.B.K2a

A METAR reports actual conditions at the time of observation (hourly, plus a SPECI whenever conditions change significantly). Elements, in order: station, date/time (UTC), wind, visibility, weather phenomena, sky condition, temperature/dew point, altimeter setting, and remarks.

📋 Worked example

Decoding a METAR

KAUS 021653Z 14012G20KT 8SM BKN035 OVC060 27/19 A2998 RMK AO2 SLP151

GroupMeaning
KAUS 021653ZAustin-Bergstrom, day 02, 1653 UTC
14012G20KTWind from 140° at 12 knots, gusting 20
8SMVisibility 8 statute miles
BKN035 OVC060Broken at 3,500 ft AGL, overcast at 6,000 ft AGL. Ceiling = 3,500 ft (lowest broken or overcast layer)
27/19Temperature 27°C, dew point 19°C
A2998Altimeter 29.98 in. Hg
Ceiling rule: the ceiling is the lowest broken or overcast layer, or vertical visibility (VV) into an obscuration. FEW and SCT layers never make a ceiling.

🔮 TAF: a forecast IR.I.B.K2c

A TAF forecasts conditions at an airport for 24 or 30 hours (issued four times a day), using the same coding as a METAR for the base forecast and then modifying groups for changes:

GroupMeaning
FM (from)A complete, rapid change at a stated time. Everything before it is superseded.
BECMG (becoming)A gradual, lasting change over a stated time range.
TEMPOTemporary fluctuations expected for less than an hour at a time, and covering less than half of the stated period.
PROB30A 30% chance of the stated conditions during the stated period. (PROB40 appears in some international TAFs.)

P6SM in a TAF means visibility greater than 6 statute miles.

🔮 Worked example

TEMPO and the alternate decision

FM021900 21015G25KT P6SM SCT040 TEMPO 0219/0221 4SM -RA BKN015

From 1900Z the base forecast is wind 210° at 15 gusting 25, visibility greater than 6 SM, scattered at 4,000. Between 1900Z and 2100Z, temporary periods of 4 SM in light rain with a broken layer at 1,500 ft.

Your ETA is 2030Z, so the 1-2-3 window runs from 1930Z to 2130Z.

The TEMPO group sits inside your window, and its 1,500-ft ceiling is below 2,000 ft, so an alternate is required even though the base forecast looks fine. Visibility (4 SM) meets the 3 SM requirement; the ceiling is what trips the rule. Any qualifying group inside the window counts, not just the prevailing forecast.
Self-check
  1. In sky condition coding, which layer defines the ceiling?
  2. What is the difference between a TEMPO and a BECMG group in a TAF?
  3. For the 1-2-3 rule, does a TEMPO group inside your ETA window count toward the alternate decision?
Show answers
  1. The lowest broken or overcast layer (or vertical visibility into an obscuration). Scattered and few layers do not count.
  2. TEMPO is a temporary fluctuation expected for less than an hour at a time within a stated period; BECMG is a gradual, lasting change over a stated time range.
  3. Yes. Any qualifying forecast group inside the window counts, not just the prevailing (base) forecast.

AIRMET, SIGMET, and PIREP

Topics: AWH Chapter 26 · About 70 min
Learning objectives
  • State what each AIRMET and SIGMET category covers and how long each is valid
  • Explain how a PIREP fits into the weather picture

⚠️ AIRMETs: Sierra, Tango, Zulu IR.I.B.K2g

AIRMETs cover conditions significant to smaller GA aircraft but less severe than a SIGMET:

AIRMETCovers
SierraIFR conditions (widespread ceilings below 1,000 ft and/or visibility below 3 SM) and mountain obscuration
TangoModerate turbulence, sustained surface winds of 30 knots or more, and non-convective low-level wind shear
ZuluModerate icing, with freezing-level information
G-AIRMET replaced the text bulletins
As of January 27, 2025, the textual Sierra/Tango/Zulu bulletins were retired for the contiguous US. The same hazard categories now come as the graphical G-AIRMET (forecast snapshots at roughly 3-hour steps out to 12 hours). Alaska and Hawaii still issue text AIRMETs.

🚨 SIGMETs, Convective SIGMETs, and CWAs

ProductCoversValid
SIGMETNon-convective hazards significant to all aircraft: severe icing, severe or extreme turbulence, dust or sandstorms, volcanic ash4 hours (6 for a hurricane)
Convective SIGMETThunderstorm hazards: severe or embedded thunderstorms, lines of thunderstorms, hail, tornadoes2 hours; issued hourly at 55 minutes past the hour
CWA (Center Weather Advisory)A short-fuse, local product from a Center Weather Service Unit, supplementing or refining an existing advisory, or covering something not yet in oneShort (about 2 hours)

🛩️ PIREPs IR.I.B.K2a

A pilot report is the one weather product built entirely from what someone actually experienced in the air, minutes ago, rather than a forecast or a ground sensor. A routine PIREP starts with UA; an urgent one (severe turbulence, severe icing, wind shear, hail, tornado or funnel cloud, volcanic ash, or any other hazard) starts with UUA. Filing your own PIREPs is part of being a good IFR citizen: forecasters cannot observe cloud tops, icing, or ride quality directly.

🧭 Worked example

Tango and a Convective SIGMET on the same route

A G-AIRMET Tango covers your route for moderate turbulence, and a Convective SIGMET was just issued for an embedded thunderstorm cluster along the same line.

The AIRMET says expect a bumpy ride generally. The Convective SIGMET is the more urgent, more specific, shorter-fuse warning about actual thunderstorms, and it should carry more weight in an immediate go/no-go or reroute decision. IR.I.B.R2b covers the limits of these products.
Self-check
  1. What does AIRMET Zulu cover?
  2. How long is a standard (non-hurricane) SIGMET valid, versus a Convective SIGMET?
  3. What changed about how AIRMETs are issued in the contiguous US as of January 2025?
  4. What prefix marks an urgent PIREP?
Show answers
  1. Moderate icing conditions, with freezing-level information.
  2. A SIGMET is valid 4 hours (6 for a hurricane); a Convective SIGMET is valid 2 hours and issued hourly at 55 minutes past the hour.
  3. The text Sierra/Tango/Zulu bulletins were retired and replaced by the graphical G-AIRMET; the same hazard categories are shown graphically.
  4. UUA (a routine PIREP uses UA).

Day 3

Forecasts, Charts, and Briefings: Month 2 Checkpoint

Topics: AWH Chapters 27–28, checkpoint · About 90 min
Learning objectives
  • Identify the major forecast and analysis products used in preflight planning
  • Consolidate Month 2 (weather) before moving to systems and navigation

🗺️ Products the ACS names IR.I.B.K2b IR.I.B.K2d IR.I.B.K2e IR.I.B.K2f

Surface analysis chart

Current fronts, pressure centers, and station observations. An analysis, not a forecast.

Ceiling and visibility chart (CVA)

A quick look at where it is IFR, MVFR, and VFR right now.

Graphical Forecasts for Aviation (GFA)

The graphical forecast tool: clouds, visibility, icing, turbulence, winds, and more, as time-stepped maps.

Winds and temperatures aloft (FB)

Forecast wind and temperature at set altitudes. Coded DDff±TT: direction in tens of degrees, speed in knots, temperature in °C. 9900 means light and variable. Speeds of 100 knots or more add 50 to the direction code (7715 is 270° at 115 knots).

Convective outlook (AC)

Issued by the Storm Prediction Center: where thunderstorms are likely, and how severe, for the coming days.

Area Forecast Discussion (AFD)

A forecaster's plain-language explanation of their reasoning and confidence. Genuinely useful reading, not just data.

Older products you may still see
Prognostic (“prog”) charts, the weather depiction chart, and the radar summary chart have largely been replaced by GFA and the ceiling and visibility chart, but they still turn up in test questions and older study material. Recognize what each one shows.

Self-check
  1. What is an Area Forecast Discussion, and why is it worth reading beyond the coded products?
  2. Which chart shows current fronts, pressure centers, and station observations, as opposed to a forecast?
  3. Which is more hazardous, rime ice or clear ice, and why?
  4. What thunderstorm stage produces the most severe turbulence and microburst potential?
  5. How long is a Convective SIGMET valid, and how often is it issued?
Show answers
  1. It is the forecaster's own plain-language explanation of their reasoning and confidence, giving context the coded METAR/TAF/AIRMET products do not.
  2. The surface analysis chart.
  3. Clear ice; it is smooth, harder to remove, and harder to see, forming from larger supercooled droplets that spread before freezing.
  4. The mature stage, where updrafts and downdrafts exist side by side.
  5. Valid 2 hours, issued hourly at 55 minutes past the hour.
